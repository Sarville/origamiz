## What shipped this session (4 commits)

1. **`b7d21ffb` — Fix tunnel receivers getting overwritten mid-chain and bending in place.**
   Two bugs in `findBeltPath`'s reconstruction/search:
   - The prior session's fix only guarded the *goal* tile being a tunnel receiver; a receiver anywhere else in the middle of the chain got a duplicate, conflicting plain entry pushed right after it, silently overwritten once `placePath` applied entries in order. Generalized the guard to any `node.viaTunnel`.
   - The deeper bug: a tunnel receiver's `ItemEjector` is a single fixed slot matching its own rotation — it can never curve. The search let the very next step off a receiver bend anyway, planning a belt the receiver would never actually feed (confirmed via `UndergroundBelt.pendingItems` piling up with the "next" tile stuck at 0 items). Added a `mustContinueStraight` guard so a bend has to land at least one real belt tile past any receiver, falling back to routing around when there's no room.
   Verified live: reproduced the pre-fix broken structures, confirmed the fix, watched real items flow through an extractor → belt → tunnel → forced-straight tile → bend chain with no stalls or orphaned tiles.

2. **`44f8e5cf` — Fix belt drag silently crossing its own loop with no tunnel.**
   `isTileBlockedForBelt`'s `exemptPaths` exempted a belt tile from blocking purely by `BeltPath` membership, with **no distance check at all** — so once a drag's start or end merely happened to sit anywhere on a large loop, the *entire* loop became fair game to walk straight across, no matter how far that crossing was from either anchor. A drag reaching into a point enclosed by its own loop crossed the loop's far wall in one step with no tunnel, silently overwriting it.
   Replaced `exemptPaths` with a `touchesAnchor` check (the tile itself, or orthogonally adjacent to `from`/`to`) — keeps the smooth merge/reshape behavior right at the anchor, but a same-chain tile anywhere else is now treated like any other foreign belt: blocked, tunnelled under if geometrically possible.
   Verified live: a same-chain tile far from either anchor now correctly reports blocked (previously didn't); a drag crossing a loop's own far wall now plans a proper tunnel pair around it, leaving the rest of the loop untouched.

3. **`69413f00` — Let belt paths start from and end into a building's own I/O.**
   `findBeltPath` treated every real building tile as an unconditional obstacle — dragging a belt to end on a stacker's input, or starting one from a building's output tile, was rejected outright even when a matching acceptor/ejector was right there.
   Added `buildingAcceptorFeeds`/`buildingEjectorLaunch` (reads world-space feed/launch tiles and directions straight off the building's own `ItemAcceptor`/`ItemEjector` slots) and split `findBeltPath` into a thin wrapper (substitutes `from`/`to` for the real launch/feed tile when either lands on a building, trying every acceptor slot in turn for multi-input buildings like a stacker) over the renamed search body (`findBeltPathSearch`, now threading `forcedStartIncoming`/`forcedEndOutgoing` through). Also closed a tunnel-landing gap this opened (a receiver landing exactly on a feed tile now has to match the required direction).
   **Got the acceptor math backwards on the first pass** — subtracted the slot's direction vector instead of adding it, used the direction as-is instead of inverting it — silently planning the feed tile on the *wrong side* of the building. Caught by cross-referencing `GameLogic.getEjectorsAndAcceptorsAtTile` (the real belt auto-orientation's own authoritative source) instead of re-deriving it a second time from intuition, and independently cross-checked via `MetaBeltBuilding.computeOptimalDirectionAndRotationVariantAtTile`.
   Verified live: a real miner piped all the way to a trash can's real acceptor, with the trash can actually consuming arriving items (queue length dropping, not growing) — confirms both directions end to end. A parallel stacker test placed a structurally correct route but the stacker never visibly consumed anything, traced to its own `inputsPerCharge: 2` needing both inputs filled first — an unrelated existing game mechanic, not a routing bug.
