## 5. The real root cause: buildings had zero margin from the tile edge

User then found the actual root cause behind both the earlier belt-curb-
color complaint AND a fresh "buildings are bigger than the tiles"
complaint (3 screenshots of `balancer`'s compact merger/splitter variant,
confirmed via `AskUserQuestion` rather than guessed - wires-layer
buildings were a false lead I chased first and ruled out live). Proof,
in order:
1. A side-by-side of the vanilla original extractor sprite vs ours showed
   the original sits ~9px in from its own canvas edge; ours touches the
   edge with zero margin.
2. Enabled `globalConfig.debug.showEntityBounds` (temporarily uncommented
   in the gitignored `config.local.js`, reverted after) - draws the
   entity's *real* logical tile-space bounds as a red rectangle, straight
   from engine code (`StaticMapEntity.getTileSpaceBounds()`), not a
   screenshot measurement. For `balancer-merger` (1 tile) and `mixer`
   (2 tiles) both, the red box matches the grid exactly - confirming the
   grid/logic side was never broken, only the *art* had no margin inside
   its own correct-size canvas.
3. User's own manual test (scaled a building down 1.125x in an image
   editor, dropped it net to the original) confirmed a smaller silhouette
   fits the grid with clean gaps - but flagged that naive image scaling
   also thins/blurs the curb bands, which they didn't want.

**Fix: regenerate through the procedural engine with the margin baked into
the geometry, not a post-hoc raster scale** (post-hoc scaling was tried
and rejected - it shrinks curb thickness along with everything else,
losing crispness). Added `MARGIN_PX = 9` to `build_platform.py` (flat px,
every side, matches the measured vanilla reference) and threaded it
through every place a platform/curb/post coordinate assumed the canvas's
own edge was the platform's edge:
- `build_platform.py`'s `build_platform_pinched` (rotator): now builds the
  whole platform+curb+posts in an inset *working* canvas
  (`W-2*MARGIN_PX*SS` etc.), then pastes that into the true-size canvas
  offset by `MARGIN_PX` - curb thickness/banding stays pixel-identical,
  only the outer position changes.
- `build_batch.py`'s generic engine (`_silhouette`, `_belt_opening_rect`,
  `_flank_patches`, `_auto_flank_posts`, `_corners`, `_free_side_mid_posts`)
  - all shifted so the platform silhouette (and everything derived from
  its distance-transform: the curb ring, belt-opening cutouts, flank
  rails) stays `MARGIN_PX` clear of the true tile edge, even at belt
  connections. This *supersedes* the earlier "seam problem" work from
  2026-08-27 that deliberately made a building's curb reach the exact
  true tile edge at connections to match the belt's curb pixel-for-pixel
  - user's explicit call: with the new margin, nothing (not even at
  connections) reaches the true edge except the direction arrow, which
  bridges the gap instead. `_arrow`/`apply_arrows` were deliberately left
  untouched - the arrow is the one thing that's *supposed* to poke past
  the margin toward the true edge.
- Roughly a dozen hand-written `SPECS` entries (`balancer`, `cutter`,
  `stacker`, `painter`, `miner`, `trash`, `underground_belt_*`, the
  `-tier2`/`-chainable` variants) had post positions as raw literal

## 6. §5 follow-up: connections don't get the margin, only free sides do

Live testing of §5's fix immediately surfaced a real regression: at belt
connections (`miner`'s top, `balancer`'s top/bottom, etc.) the curb now
stopped `MARGIN_PX` short of the true edge same as free sides - but the
belt itself still reaches the true edge with its *own* separate ~13.5px
inherent margin (`beltBorder-curbWidth` in `generate_belt_sprites.js`).
Stacking both margins at a connection roughly doubled the visible gap
there, which read as the two curbs "spreading apart"/disconnected rather
than meeting - user caught this immediately on the very first live
placement (`miner`), then separately flagged multi-tile buildings
(`balancer`) as "still large", which turned out to be the same root
cause (a mix of correctly-margined free sides and still-touching
connected sides reading as visually inconsistent overall).

**Fix: reverted every belt-connection-side change from §5**
(`_belt_opening_rect`, `_flank_patches`'s Y-bounds, `_auto_flank_posts`)
back to reaching the true tile edge, exactly like before `MARGIN_PX`
existed - connections are simply not part of this margin fix at all.
Also removed `_flank_patches`'s `mask &= plat` line entirely (it was
silently re-clipping the reverted flank rails back to the now-smaller
inset silhouette regardless of the Y-bound revert, since `plat` is
itself margin-inset - the rail needs to be able to extend past the
silhouette's own edge at a connection, so it can no longer be masked to
it).

**The remaining, correct problem**: `_silhouette`'s base-rectangle inset
needs to be *per-side*, not uniform - a side gets `MARGIN_PX` only if
it's 100% free (no belt connection anywhere along it), 0 if it carries
any connection. Reworked `_silhouette(..., belt_edges=())` to compute an
independent inset per edge from `{e for e, _ in belt_edges}`. Corners are
shared between two sides that can now disagree (e.g. `miner`'s top-left
corner sits between a connected top and a free left) - `_corners(...,
belt_edges=())` now computes the x-offset and y-offset independently
from whichever two sides meet there, instead of one shared `INSET`
value. Threaded `belt_edges` through every call site: automatic for the
~30 buildings built via the `_mk()` helper (already had `belt_edges` in
scope), manual for the dozen hand-written `SPECS` entries (`balancer`,
`cutter`, `stacker`, `painter`, `miner`, `trash`, `underground_belt_*`
and their `-tier2`/`-chainable` variants) - each now passes its own
`belt_edges` list into its `_corners()` call.

Regenerated and re-copied the same 28 buildings, re-ran blueprints.

**Unrelated infrastructure snag hit along the way**: `imgres.buildAtlas`
started intermittently failing ("Java not found / unsupported version?")
and `imgres.atlasToJson` then hard-errored (`Cannot read properties of

session's cumulative asset additions (59 new UI icons + toolbar panel +
all these building regenerations) finally pushed the "hq" atlas past a
single 2048x2048 texture page, and `atlas2json.js` has a hard "supports
only one page" limitation (a `lever` sprite had spilled onto a tiny

`maxWidth` from 2048 to 4096 (kept `maxHeight` at 2048 - the overflow
was tiny, plenty of headroom) - back to a single page, `atlasToJson`
succeeded. Worth knowing for any future asset-heavy session: if
`atlasToJson` ever throws that same `split` error again, check for a
second `.atlas`/page file before assuming it's transient.

## 7. §6 was wrong: margin belongs on every side, including connections — and it scales per tile

User caught this with a precise measurement, not a vague "still looks
off": screenshotted `miner` in-editor and measured its visible-content
box at exactly **174×183** starting at position (9,0) inside the 192×192
canvas. Three sides (the ones that were 174 wide/tall) sat flush inside
their grid cell; the fourth (183) did not. Conclusion, stated by the user
and confirmed by re-checking the very first vanilla-reference screenshot
from §5: the true playable grid cell is **174×174 in every direction**,
full stop — §6's "connections don't get a margin" theory was wrong. The
belt's own ~13.5px connector margin is a separate, pre-existing thing
that already reaches the true edge on the belt side; it doesn't mean the
*building* curb should also touch the true edge there. Reverted §6's
per-side logic back to a uniform inset on all four sides (`_silhouette`,
`_corners`, `_belt_opening_rect`, `_flank_patches`, `_auto_flank_posts`
all take one inset again, connections included) — `_arrow`/`apply_arrows`
still deliberately untouched, the arrow remains the only thing allowed to
cross into the margin.

Two more rounds of user-caught bugs followed directly from that revert:

**7a. Flank rail "jog".** Screenshots of a vertical flank rail (the
repeating post/rail strip along a building's free long side) showed it
running straight from the bottom, then jogging sideways partway up —
mirrored on the opposite rail. Cause: `_flank_patches`/
`_auto_flank_posts` build a rail as two pieces — a segment anchored to
the (now margin-shifted) curb ring, and a segment built out to the *true*
tile edge — and only the first piece's column position had been updated
for the new inset; the second piece was still computed from the old
unmargined `CURB_INSET` constant, so the two pieces met at different x
(or y) coordinates. Fixed by adding `lmg`/`rmg` (and `tmg`/`bmg`) column
shifts to `_flank_patches` and `_auto_flank_posts`, applied only on the
flank nearest a true tile edge (`offset==0` or `offset+TILE==w/h`) so a
mid-run rail on a multi-tile building isn't shifted where there's no true
edge to align to.

**7b. Margin doesn't scale with tile count.** User's sharpest catch:
`cutter.png` is 384×192 (2 tiles wide, 1 tall) and the flat `MARGIN_PX=9`
left far too little gap on the width axis — a 2-tile-wide building needs
**18px** off each width edge, not 9, because the true tile is 174 wide
*per tile*, not 174 total. I asked one clarifying question this time
(`AskUserQuestion`, learning from over-hastily reinterpreting feedback
earlier in the session) rather than guessing again, and got the exact
rule back: "мы выяснили, что размер клетки - 174х174. постройка 1х2
тайла должна быть не больше 174х348... это и есть отступы по 9 и по 18
на высоту и ширину." I.e. `margin_per_side = (canvas_dim_px // 192) * 9`,
independently per axis.

**Fix:** added an `_mg(dim)` helper implementing that formula and
threaded it through every function that used to take the flat
`MARGIN_PX` (`_silhouette`, `_belt_opening_rect`, `_flank_patches`
(including the 7a column-shift math), `_auto_flank_posts`, `_corners`,
`_free_side_mid_posts`), each now called with the building's actual
width/height in px so a 1-tile axis gets 9 and a 2-tile axis gets 18. The
four hand-written 2-tile-wide `SPECS` entries (`balancer`, `cutter`,
`stacker`, `painter`, all 384×192) that had literal margin numbers baked
into their post coordinates got a `_MGW2 = _mg(384) = 18` alias for their
width-axis literals, keeping the existing `_MG = 9` alias for height-axis
literals.

Verified two ways before regenerating: (1) pixel bbox measurement of the
regenerated `cutter_v2.png`/`balancer_v2.png` — content box
x:17-366,y:7-186 ≈ 349×179 at position ≈(18,9), matching the
348×174-at-(18,9) target from the user's own formula; (2) live in-game
placement after a full atlas rebuild — a real `miner` (1×1) now shows a
clean, even margin on all three free sides plus the arrow poking into the
neighbor tile, and a real `balancer` (2×1, confirmed via its tooltip
after an earlier attempt accidentally placed the 1-tile
`balancer-merger` variant instead) shows its left/right curb posts flush
with the tile-grid lines two cells over, with the same top/bottom margin
as the miner. Regenerated and re-copied all 28 buildings from §5/§6 a
third time.
